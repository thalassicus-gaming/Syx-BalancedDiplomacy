Have you ever been annoyed about tiny, weak factions harassing you all the time?

My goal with Balanced Diplomacy is to keep the game as close to vanilla as possible, while making the diplomacy system more fun and challenging. Threats now come from factions who can back up their words with military force. Additionally, demands are affordable enough that you can actually make a choice, rather than having to accept penalties from impossible demands.

## Rivalry and harassment

Games are fun when you face challenges roughly equal to or stronger than your own capabilities.

**Vanilla:**
Weak factions harassed you constantly. This was due to low trust, which came from a rivalry penalty. The penalty solely depended on your relative wealth, so the poorest neighbours were always the most hostile. Army strength did not factor into this.

This forced the player to choose between boredom (trivial wars) and frustration (constant penalties). Many players opted to just raze their neighbors and abandon territory to avoid both outcomes.

A side-effect is vassals were functionally unusable, since they start with no wealth or army.

**Mod:**
Your peers now consider you rivals. Weak realms don't threaten you, and strong realms don't consider you a threat. Hostility peaks when your economic and military power is roughly equal. This provides a more fun, even challenge when rivalry leads to war.

## Peace deals

Wars are more interesting when you get the option to end it early in exchange for tribute, instead of having to completely annihilate your enemy.

**Vanilla:**
Peace deal size came from the player's wealth and the strength of all the player's neighbors, with no consideration for the specific faction you're at war with. A faction on the verge of destruction could demand you hand over half your empire, and it was impossible for the player to demand tribute.

**Mod:**
Terms now come from relative army strength and how long the war has run. NPCs start with an aggressive bluster, then become more willing to strike a deal as a war drags on. The loser pays up to 10% of all territory and wealth they can offer, and a beaten NPC can be made to pay tribute.

## "Relations worsen"

Saving a trade deal should be expensive, but possible.

**Vanilla:**
When opinion drops below the threshold necessary for a diplomatic agreement, the payment to save that agreement could literally be impossible. When the game couldn't assemble enough goods, it wrote the shortfall into credits, ignoring your actual treasury total.

**Mod:**
Offers are now built only from things you own, and partial payment earns proportional goodwill. Agreeing to a small payment will delay the cancellation of a treaty, buying you time to restore opinion.

## Low-trust threats

Threats should be backed by military force, and possible to actually agree to.

**Vanilla:**
Threats from people with low trust could demand most of your realm, including territory. There was no military consideration, and refusing carried a heavy penalty that triggered more threats.

**Mod:**
Extortion attempts are now measured against what you can actually part with. Land is off the table, and the size of the attempt scales with relative army strength.

## Weak gifts

If gifting is so weak it can't get you a deal, then it functionally doesn't exist.

**Vanilla:**
A gift of a million credits would move a mid-size faction by 0.04 opinion, when 1.5 was needed for a trade agreement. Additionally, the game forgets small gifts fastest, so every gift you could afford was in the category it quickly discards.

**Mod:**
Gifts are now in a balanced range that allows you to sway NPC opinion, without trivializing diplomacy. The same gift of one million credits buys 0.8 opinion. This change makes the other deals discussed above more affordable.

## Hidden "King's Pride" mechanic

The vanilla game had a nearly-invisible mechanic causing gift memory and demand sizes to swing up to 400% based on the "pride" of the person making the demand. This is now in the 25% range, favoring rational decision-making with *some* randomness.

# Compatibility
This mod stays as close to vanilla as possible, while tweaking diplomatic variables. This mod is safe to add to an existing save, though it will immediately change who considers you a threat, so be prepared.

The mod has no known compatibility issues. It was necessary to replace several diplomacy-related files, however, so it might not work well with overhaul or other diplomacy-related mods. If you encounter a problem, please leave a comment on the mod's Steam Workshop page.
